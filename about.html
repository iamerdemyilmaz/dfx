About this course

The author

Erdem Yilmaz is a senior manufacturing engineer with about eight years of new product introduction experience in mechanical and electromechanical products. This course is written in his voice as a practitioner: the rules are the ones he applies, and the reasons are the ones he gives when a designer asks why.

How the course was written and verified

Credibility was the design requirement. Every real-world example, statistic, and standard in the course was checked against a source before it was written, and the source is cited inline and listed in the module's references with the date it was accessed. Where a source could be read in full, it is listed without qualification. Where a source could only be confirmed through a credible secondary account, the reference is marked "secondary". Claims that could not be verified were either removed or rewritten as what they are: widely repeated rules of thumb whose original data is hard to trace.

Numeric design rules come mainly from the published design guides of manufacturers and service bureaus, from standards bodies' catalogue pages, and from industry association documents. They are paraphrased, not copied, and every diagram is drawn for this course. Values differ between suppliers, and the course says so where it matters.

Examples marked "illustrative" use invented but realistic numbers and are not real programs. Real cases are named and cited to public sources such as accident investigation reports, court and licensing records, regulators' pages, company statements, and peer-reviewed or institutional publications. The course contains no information about the author's employers, past or present.

The consolidated bibliography is on the references page. The working register behind it, with a verification status for each source, is kept in the course repository as SOURCES.md.

What the course is not

It is an educational resource, not engineering advice for a specific product. Design rules are starting points that depend on material, supplier, and application; standards change revision; regulations differ by market. Confirm anything you rely on with your supplier, the current edition of the standard, and, where safety or compliance is involved, a qualified professional.
